The 5 Days

Five STEAM disciplines.
Five mission days.

Every day your kiddo steps into a new mission role and tackles a new STEAM discipline — coordinate math on Monday, atmospheric science on Tuesday, coding and AI on Wednesday, world-building on Thursday, and rocket engineering on Friday. Each day's work feeds directly into the next. By Friday, your kiddo has done five real things, built five real projects, and earned the kind of confidence that only comes from finishing something genuinely hard.

Day 1 · Math · Navigator
Signal Lock — Where did it come from?

The transmission lands at 9AM Monday. Three coordinate pings, one source. Teams tear open sealed envelopes, sprint to the 10×10 floor star chart, and start running yarn lines across the room. Where all three meet — that's the planet. That star goes on the wall Monday morning and doesn't come down until Friday. Every day starts by pointing at it.

AI Integration

Campers ask an AI what a real space agency would do with three signal coordinates — then compare it to what they just did themselves. Spoiler: they're already doing the real thing.

Skills developed
Coordinate plottingTriangulationSpatial reasoningTeam decision-making
🔭
Day 2 · Science · Science Officer
Spectrum Scan — What's the air like there?

Before we can launch a rescue pod, we need to know what's in the atmosphere. So your kiddo builds a spectroscope — a real optical instrument — from scratch, calibrates it against the classroom lights, then rotates through four stations analyzing alien atmospheric gas samples. By end of day, they've written a science-backed mission recommendation. A 7-year-old. With evidence.

AI Integration

Teams describe their gas findings to an AI and ask it to predict habitability. Then they compare it to their own evidence-backed conclusion — and catch what the AI got wrong.

Skills developed
Instrument buildingGas identificationObservation & recordingEvidence-based reasoning
🤖
Day 3 · Technology + AI · Communications Officer
Alien Decoder — What are they saying?

The signal has words in it. Your kiddo's team invents a 12-symbol alien language, writes a distress message in it, then builds a working Scratch translator — pressing a key makes an alien sprite speak the symbol out loud. Then they teach the same language to an AI and watch it try to decode another team's message. The Scratch program never gets it wrong. The AI definitely does. And that conversation — why does code always work but AI sometimes fails? — is the best part of Wednesday.

AI Integration

Teach the AI your symbol key. Ask it to translate a rival team's message. Compare word by word. Diagnose the errors. Improve the examples. That's real AI engineering — at age 8.

Skills developed
Scratch block codingLanguage designAI few-shot learningCritical AI evaluation
🌍
Day 4 · Arts · Worldbuilder
World Build — Who lives there?

We know the planet. We know the atmosphere. We know the language. Thursday is for the question that's been building all week: WHO is on the other end of that signal? Teams build a full 3D alien civilization across four stations — terrain painted using Day 2's atmospheric colors, creatures built from clay, artifacts carved in Day 3's symbols, a civilization flag in their own alien language. The diorama has to cite its sources. Every choice needs evidence. Art meets science on Thursday.

AI Integration

Campers describe their alien civilization to an AI image generator and compare the output to the diorama they built by hand. What did the AI get right? What could it not possibly have known?

Skills developed
Character designEvidence-based artCollaborative buildingVisual storytelling
🚀
Day 5 · Engineering · Mission Engineer
Rescue Launch + Family Showcase

Friday. Launch day. Every camper designs and builds their own stomp rocket, loads the rescue pod (friendship message in alien language, mini star map from Day 1, symbolic gift), and takes it outside for test launches. Then families arrive — and the 30-minute parallel showcase begins. Four teams present simultaneously. All 20 campers launch their rockets simultaneously. And each coach delivers one personal standout moment for each camper, out loud, in front of their family. This is the moment. And it is worth every single day that built to it.

AI Integration

Before the launch, campers ask an AI to predict flight distance from their rocket's design specs. After the launch, they compare the AI's prediction to the chalk-mark on the ground. Then they explain what the AI couldn't have accounted for that they figured out with their hands.

Skills developed
Rocket engineeringBuild-test-iteratePresenting to an audienceMission thinking
